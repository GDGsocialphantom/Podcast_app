A furniture maker who couldn't sew sold 15,000 face masks in eight weeks and discovered the masks weren't the business.

On this episode of The Disruption Lab, host Kevin McGinnis sits down with John Pryor, founder of Madison Stitch and Madison Flitch in Kansas City's East Crossroads.

Pryor had just opened a newly renovated studio in January 2020 when COVID hit. Mask orders flooded in, and to fill them he had to look outward. About 45 of the stitchers who arrived came through organizations like Catholic Charities, Jewish Vocational Services, and Rightfully Sewn, many from Burma and Afghanistan. Their sewing skills had been passed down for generations, but they had been working warehouse and factory jobs where none of it showed. When the Great Resignation pulled workers away to $30-an-hour jobs at Amazon, Walmart, and FedEx, Pryor rebuilt again, this time around materials: locally sourced leather, wood, and solid brass.

The conversation goes inside the model: royalty contracts for designers, lifetime guarantees on every bag, a studio he owns outright with no investors, partners, or bank debt, and a customer base of architects, engineers, and builders rather than fast-fashion shoppers. Pryor is also candid about the winding path that got him there (degrees in history, theology, and business, three years tagging religious literature in a cubicle, and burnout), and about being called a fashion designer while still feeling like a poser.
