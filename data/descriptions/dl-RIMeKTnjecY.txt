What does it take to rename a street that still functions as Kansas City's color line — and what happens when the city's own mayor won't let it happen?

On this episode of The Disruption Lab, recorded live in front of a studio audience, host Kevin McGinnis sits down with Chris Goode, founder of Ruby Jean's Juicery, the first 100% health-focused food business ever to open on Kansas City's East Side.

Goode traces the story from its origin: at 14, he lost his great-grandmother to a sudden heart attack and his grandmother, Ruby Jean, to type 2 diabetes — three days apart — and made a vow that would take a decade, a stable insurance career in Chicago, and a 10-day juice cleanse in Los Angeles to finally act on. He explains why, in 2017, he chose to open a Ruby Jean's location directly on Troost Avenue, the historic redlining boundary named for a man who enslaved six people, instead of avoiding the address — and how that decision built the trust that later got Ruby Jean's discovered, unsolicited, by a Whole Foods employee who had spent months secret-shopping every location before ever introducing herself.

The conversation moves through the systems Goode has built since: Truth Over Troost, his campaign to rename the avenue, now stalled at the mayor's desk despite roughly 15,000 petition signatures; the first bus-stop adoption program in Kansas City history; a Kansas City Public Schools juice pilot that reached three schools and 10,000 bottles before it was never renewed; and the reality of scaling a mission-driven brand into national CPG distribution while, eleven years in, still chasing capital and cash flow. Goode is candid about what breaks in that transition, why Whole Foods will "like you but not love you" until the bottles move, and why he believes real change for kids in under-resourced schools will only come when wealthy institutions decide to care.

Chris Goode is the founder of Ruby Jean's Juicery, a Kansas City-based juice and wellness brand carried in more than 160 Whole Foods locations nationwide. Kevin McGinnis is CEO of the Keystone Innovation District in Kansas City.
