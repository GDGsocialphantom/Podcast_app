What if one of the cheapest clean energy sources in the world is sitting under Kansas, and nobody knows yet how to get it out?

On part two of this live Keystone Session, Brendan Bream breaks down geologic hydrogen. This is hydrogen that forms naturally underground, and it is quickly becoming one of the most closely watched exploration plays in energy. Pulling it straight from the ground could cost about half as much as making hydrogen from natural gas, and that process also releases CO2. Venture capital is pouring in, more than 100 companies are chasing it, and Kansas has become a global hotspot for exploration. Yet only one place in the world actually produces geologic hydrogen today: a field in Mali that was discovered by accident while drilling a water well.

Bream draws on his background in the oil and gas industry to separate the hype from the physics. He explains why Kansas has geologic success but not yet economic success, and why the hydrogen found so far is dissolved in salt water rather than sitting in a free-flowing pocket of gas. He also places the whole industry on the Gartner hype cycle: cresting the peak of inflated expectations and likely heading into the trough of disillusionment.
