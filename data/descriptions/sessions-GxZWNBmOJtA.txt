What does it take to go from "here's roughly where the utilities are" to breaking ground in 30 days?

In part 1 of 3 of this Keystone Sessions takeover, JE Dunn Construction's Evan Fox (Principal Architect, Design Phase Services) and Dylan Lowder (VDC Director, self-perform/concrete) pull back the curtain on how a 100-year-old, 6,000-person general contractor is re-architecting its own process to keep up with the speed of mission-critical construction — data centers in particular, where clients are now hiring JE Dunn in July and breaking ground in August.

Fox explains why JE Dunn built an in-house design phase services group of architects and engineers embedded inside a construction company — founded, he says, on the idea that architects understand more about construction than contractors understand about design — and how the shift from traditional and CM-at-risk delivery to design-build has pulled contractors earlier and earlier into the design process. Lowder then walks through VDC (Virtual Design and Construction): building every project as a full 3D model before ground is broken, using Autodesk Forma as the single source of truth for RFIs, submittals, and contract documents across a project, and Trimble Connect to put those models directly in the hands of field crews — down to checking footing widths against an iPad in the trench.

The conversation moves through what it takes to scale that discipline across 400 active projects and 52 national VDC resources, why self-perform work (JE Dunn's own tradespeople, not subcontractors) is central to controlling quality and schedule, and the real challenge of enterprise technology: implementation is easy, adoption is hard.
