What does it actually take to keep creative talent in the city where it was born?

In this episode, Jo Blaq — Grammy-nominated music producer, songwriter, and founder of The Distrkct KC — joins host Kevin McGinnis to unpack what it really means to build creative infrastructure in a city that has always produced world-class talent but rarely retained the credit.

Jo breaks down what Grammy-level music production actually looks like behind the scenes — the sleepless sessions, the counseling, the sociology — and why those skills translate far beyond the studio. He talks about what he saw when he came back to Kansas City after years in LA working with Ariana Grande, Justin Bieber, and Jill Scott, and why what he saw made him stay. And he walks through what The District is building in the Crossroads: a multimedia hub with professional-grade studios, a content creation space, membership access, and a pathway for creatives to build careers — not just portfolios — without leaving home.

They also get into AI and technology's role in democratizing creative work, why creative infrastructure belongs in the same conversation as economic development, and what it would mean for 20 Kansas City artists to walk the Grammy red carpet together.

If you've heard Kansas City called a "hidden gem" for creative talent but never understood what's actually been missing — the spaces, the equipment, the career pipelines, the community — this is the episode.
