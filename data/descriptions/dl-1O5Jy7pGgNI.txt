Most people see a broken system and look away. Max Kaniger walked into 70 corner stores before a single one said yes — and built Kanbe's Markets into a 125-location operation feeding 260 square miles of Kansas City.

In this conversation, host Kevin McGinnis and Max go deep on the real mechanics of food access: why the food desert map and the redlining map look identical, what happened on the ground when federal food programs were pulled back, and why Dollar General sells more groceries than Whole Foods.

They cover:
— Why the corner store was the answer, not the problem
— Operating inside redlined geography
— The immediate, on-the-ground impact of pulled food program funding
— What cities get wrong about food access
— Breaking Google Sheets at 700,000 lines - Running a full supply chain on spreadsheets
— The four-tier produce system - How Kanbe's sorts 1.7 million pounds of rescued food across stores, nonprofits, animal farms, and compost
— Walking away from a seven-figure contract
— Food as medicine and what's next

Max Kaniger is the founder of Kanbe's Markets, a Kansas City-based nonprofit reimagining how food reaches the communities that traditional grocery has abandoned.
