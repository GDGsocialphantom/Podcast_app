How does a metro area ranked 33rd in the country outcompete ten cities in the top fifteen for World Cup host status and then get told by FIFA that it redefined the job?

On this episode of The Disruption Lab, host Kevin McGinnis sits down with Cliff Illig: co-founder of Cerner, majority owner of Sporting Kansas City, and co-chair of the KC2026 World Cup Host Committee, in a conversation recorded live at a Kansas City exhibit on sports architecture and design.

Illig traces the real timeline behind Kansas City becoming a 2026 FIFA World Cup host city: the failed 1994 bid, the promise Lamar Hunt extracted from Illig and his partners before selling them the team, and the decade of soccer-specific infrastructure. Stadiums, training facilities, a new airport, all things that made Kansas City's bid credible against metros four times its size. He explains how Kansas City ended up hosting four World Cup base camps, including Argentina, England, and the Netherlands, when most host cities got none, and how FIFA rewrote its own rulebook mid-process to approve a second Kansas City training facility it hadn't originally planned to use.

From there, the conversation moves past the tournament itself into what sustained impact actually looks like once the games end, the business case for keeping Major League sports franchises in a region, why design and lifestyle became Kansas City's real competitive advantage in recruiting national talent, and why he believes Kansas City no longer has to defend its ability to host anything.
