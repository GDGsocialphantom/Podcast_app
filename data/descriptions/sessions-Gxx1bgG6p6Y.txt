Insect ecologist Dan Marczzek breaks down how university natural history collections and museum specimens are used to track, protect, and conserve endangered butterfly species from California's Hermes copper and Harbison's dun skipper to Missouri's own Linda's roadside skipper and regal fritillary.

In this Keystone Sessions talk, Dan explains why so many butterflies depend on just a single host plant to survive, how researchers use historic museum records and citizen science tools like iNaturalist and Calflora to relocate "lost" populations, and how his own fieldwork is feeding data back into the collections that made it possible.
