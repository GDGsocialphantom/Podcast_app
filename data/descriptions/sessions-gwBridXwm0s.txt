What if the piece you wore into a room did the introducing for you?

This is part one of two of this live Keystone Session where sculptural jewelry designer Clarissa Knighten, owner of Rissa’s Artistic Design, makes the case for jewelry as wearable art. She builds pieces from shaped tree branches, stones, pearls, cowrie shells, fabric and even bike chain. Her talk, “Breaking the Traditional Rules of Balance,” isn’t about work-life balance. It’s about the rules for how jewelry is supposed to look, fit and be worn, and what happens when you ignore them.

Clarissa explains how one piece on the runway at the 2019 West 18th Street Fashion Show led to a meeting with the Museum of Kansas City. That meeting became a 17-by-5-foot ceiling tapestry, installed in 2021 and expanded in 2023, and she had never attempted anything like it before. She also shares how her piece “Spiritual Awakening,” shown at the Nelson-Atkins Museum of Art with the African American Artists Collective, ended up on a banner on Emanuel Cleaver II Boulevard. And she tells how the Mulvane Art Museum in Topeka bought a piece for its permanent collection without ever seeing it in person.
