Travis Fitzwater spent 12 years as a Missouri state legislator passing laws to support entrepreneurs. Now he's leaving the Senate to actually run the state's venture fund. In this live episode, host Kevin McGinnis digs into why founders have no voice in the capitol, how a state-backed fund evaluates risk differently than traditional VCs, and what it takes to sell innovation as economic development in a state caught between two coasts.

They also talk about rural entrepreneurship, AI in agriculture, the bipartisan case for startup investment, and the personal cost of public service — including the night his seven-year-old daughter asked him not to leave.

We cover: 
 – Why a senator walked away from reelection
 – Serving multiple masters: MTC, the legislature, and the governor
 – Why entrepreneurs don't have lobbyists
 – Walking back into the chamber after a zeroed-out budget
 – The Right to Start Act: 117–7 in the House
 – Missouri vs. the coasts: the case for staying home
 – How MTC's Idea Fund evaluates startups differently
 – The honest failure rate of a state venture fund
 – From side hustle guy to MTC leader
 – AI, ag tech, and the state's role in innovation
 – Can MTC help modernize government itself?
 – Workforce development and untapped potential
 – Leadership philosophy: service over self
 – What the community can do for MTC
 – The through line: "We were on a mission"
