What if the next energy resource in Kansas isn’t oil or gas, but hydrogen that has been building up underground the whole time?

Today we bring you part one of three of this live Keystone Session where Brendan Bream, a geologist with the Kansas Geological Survey and roughly 20 years in the energy industry, explains why Kansas may be sitting on an energy opportunity few people saw coming. His career took him from ExxonMobil to a faculty post at Vanderbilt and to helicopter-supplied drilling sites in the mountains of Papua New Guinea. He came to Kansas expecting a quiet job teaching the next generation of geologists, since the state’s oil production had peaked back in the ’60s and ’70s. Then geologic hydrogen came up.

Bream describes how the Kansas Geological Survey works. It is the second-largest state geological survey in the country, it turns every state dollar into about three federal research dollars, and it has put a team of former oil and gas scientists to work on a new question: is there usable hydrogen under Kansas, and could the state’s salt deposits store hydrogen like a battery?
