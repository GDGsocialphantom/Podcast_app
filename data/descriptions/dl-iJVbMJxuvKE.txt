What if the reason kids struggle socially isn't a deficit — it's just that no one ever gave them a safe place to practice?

In this episode of The Disruption Lab, host Kevin McGinnis sits down with Dr. Amber Rowland (University of Kansas researcher and co-founder) and Paul Epp (serial entrepreneur and co-founder) to unpack the origin, science, and business of VOISS — a virtual reality platform designed to help youth with autism and social-emotional learning needs build real-world social skills through safe, private simulation.

What we cover:
Why traditional social skills training unintentionally humiliates the students it's trying to help
How 143 VR scenarios and 182 social skills were built from 25 major school assessments
The moment Amber realized this product couldn't be allowed to die like so many others
The economics of commercializing university-funded research — IP licensing, tech transfer clocks, and OSEP grants
How VOISS landed its first school district customer (Greenville, SC) without a marketing budget
Why COVID quietly erased a generation of social development
Project I Know: the AI-powered next chapter that puts students in real-time conversation with an agent
How Paul thinks about AI that teaches people to be more human — not replace them

For every dollar spent on social-emotional learning, $11 are saved in disciplinary remediation. This is what investing in the right system looks like.
