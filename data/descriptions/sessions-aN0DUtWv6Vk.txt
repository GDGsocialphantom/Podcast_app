What does it take to turn a habit of keeping your hands busy into a 19-year career, museum commissions, and a business that runs on referrals?

In part two from a live Keystone Session, sculptural jewelry designer Clarissa Knighten, owner of Rissa’s Artistic Design, answers the questions working artists and founders actually want to ask. Her career started in an elevator, when a stranger asked where she got the piece she was wearing and bought it from her on the spot. That was the signal that she could do this full-time.

Clarissa walks through how her largest works came together. The first is a 17-foot ceiling piece for the Kansas City Museum, made from Harry Lauder’s walking stick and wisteria grown on the museum’s own grounds. She and her assistant worked on it for six months before turning it over to see it from below for the first time. The second is a 13-foot vertical piece in copper and pearls for the Community Health Center in Pittsburgh, Kansas, designed with the architecture firm Helix.
