What happens when a trained sculptor decides the materials don’t matter, and the idea does?

On the second part of this Keystone Sessions, artist and fabricator Juniper Tangpuz walks through what it takes to turn an idea into a real object. He starts with life-size acrobats for the Salina Arts Festival’s 50th anniversary. One balances on a light post. Another hangs off a bridge, held up by a single bent pipe that runs from ankle to hand. He installed them at the last minute, after a storm tore through the park. None of it is bronze or gold. Most of it is paper, and the whole system packs down small enough to fly.

Juniper also takes us inside the University of Kansas fabrication shops in Lawrence, where he runs the CNC plasma cutter and helps students close the gap between what they imagine and what they can build. He talks about parametric design, a tree-house workshop modeled on Bjarke Ingels’ architecture, and why he hung a CNC mistake of the Parthenon Marbles on the wall instead of throwing it away.

Then the talk gets personal. He grew up in a Filipino immigrant family in Kansas City, and making things gave him a way out. He built a horse with moving legs for an audience abroad without speaking their language, and he left unsanctioned art behind in Cuba. Through all of it, his point is the same: everyone needs a place where they’re still eight years old and free to make anything.
