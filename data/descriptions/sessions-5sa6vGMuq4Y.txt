What if one of Kansas City’s biggest industries is one nobody is talking about?

Just under 33,000 people in Kansas City work in digital media. That’s close to the 35,000 in the region’s life sciences workforce. The bioscience side gets the attention, funding and civic support, while digital media mostly goes unnoticed. And 47% of these workers don’t work for media companies at all. They’re in hospitals, dental offices and nonprofits, often working alone with no peers around them.

In the final part of our Keystone Sessions talk, Ron Green, founder of KC Imagine (the Kansas City Institute for Media, Animation, and Graphic Innovation in Education) and Digistory KC, explains why digital media is one of Missouri’s best-kept economic secrets. He covers why companies are hiring faster than schools can train people, and what happens when colleges and employers stop talking to each other.

Ron also talks frankly about his own nonprofit’s struggles to survive. Grants require budgets a startup can’t reach. Schools are locked into Common Core. A registered apprenticeship program drew three firms and no takers. And Kansas City supports founders who start businesses but leaves nonprofit founders on their own. His answer to all of it was a three-way partnership with Operation Breakthrough’s Ignition Lab and the Thank You Walt Disney nonprofit at the historic Laugh-O-Gram Studio, the building at 31st and Troost where Walt Disney started his animation career.
