What if the thing you're chasing isn't actually the goal? It's not the title, the achievement, or the parking spot with your name on it.

In today's Keystone Session, speaker and author Lauren Schiffer breaks down the difference between success and significance: two words used interchangeably that actually describe two different operating systems for a life. Success, she argues, is a closed loop. Something that is focused on you, your achievements, what you can point to. Significance is an open one, measured not by what you've built for yourself but by what people say you built for them.

Schiffer traces the framework back to her father, a career Air Force colonel who spent years teaching her to read the "road signs" embedded in ordinary moments, lessons she now calls her "kernels of wisdom" and has built into two books. She introduces her Two Pillars of Serenity and Significance: self-esteem (which she distinguishes sharply from self-confidence) and respect to all people, not respect for all people, a distinction she argues most leaders get wrong. Together, she says, the two pillars function like an operating system running quietly in the background of every decision, always invisible until one of them fails.

This is a talk about what actually holds up under pressure, and why chasing significance instead of success tends to produce both.
