Starting a business: Missouri beats 74% of America. Keeping it alive: 1.7%. Victor Hwang returns with the report card.

In The Disruption Lab's first virtual episode — and its first return visit — Victor Hwang, founder and CEO of Right to Start and former VP of Entrepreneurship at the Kauffman Foundation, walks Kevin McGinnis through his organization's 2026 Missouri Entrepreneurship Profile. The data tells a story no ribbon cutting captures: strong intent, strong starts, top-quartile business formation — then a survival cliff at years one and five that ranks near the bottom of every state result recorded since 2021. 

Victor and Kevin dig into why the businesses that survive hire well while so many die early, the "deaths of a thousand cuts" behind the number, the policy page's surprise (taxes fine, education excellent — child care, health care, and red tape in the basement), Self-Employment Assistance and why most states still make the unemployed pretend to job-hunt instead of building their own companies, Right to Start's national scoreboard — 32 governors signed onto the Governors' Compact on Entrepreneurship, 150+ Founding Mayors, offices of entrepreneurship in 18 states — the Kansas City two-state laboratory, what AI job displacement means for business creation, and the Monday-morning playbook for a legislator, a mayor, and an ecosystem builder.
