Kansas City firms had a hand in 27 of the 30 NFL stadiums and all 16 FIFA World Cup stadiums. They design 23% of the world’s power systems, 10% of its transportation infrastructure and 9% of its telecom systems. So why does the rest of the country still think “barbecue and Hallmark”?

On this episode of The Disruption Lab, taped in front of a live studio audience, Kevin talks with Ashley McDonald, who leads KC Global Design at the Kansas City Area Development Council (KCADC). They get into how Kansas City became a global center for architecture, engineering and construction (AEC), and what it will take to keep that momentum going after the World Cup.

In this episode:
• Why 1,400 architecture and engineering firms, and 21 of the top 25 design firms, chose Kansas City
• How competitors like Burns & McDonnell, Black & Veatch and Populous ended up working together (“coopetition”)
• What hosting the 2026 World Cup did for KC’s design and construction industry
• The “epiphany of the visit”: why people who come to Kansas City don’t want to leave
• The NFL Design Draft parody video that was a finalist for a global City Nation Place award
• Why only about 25% of KU architecture grads stay in Kansas City, and how to change that
• Data centers, energy and KC’s chance to lead in infrastructure innovation
• The new Chiefs and Royals stadiums and the coming capacity challenge
• What will make KC the ambitious career choice for the next generation of engineers
