Why does innovation keep stalling — even when the technology works?
After more than 50 conversations with founders, CEOs, military leaders, and community builders on The Disruption Lab, Kevin McGinnis steps out from behind the interview desk for the show’s first solo episode to name the pattern underneath all of it.
The bottleneck is almost never the technology. It’s the invisible systems surrounding innovation — procurement, institutional trust, cultural readiness, regulatory frameworks — that weren’t designed to absorb change at the speed it arrives. Kevin draws on conversations from across the show’s catalog to build the case: Kyle McAllister at Trially, where AI that works in minutes faces a healthcare system that builds at the speed of trust. Erik Murray at Eastside Innovations, where billions flow into underserved communities without producing benefit because capital arrives without governance. And the CEO of Goodwill, where AI adoption stalls not because people can’t learn the tools, but because no one asked what they actually need.
Three sectors. Three completely different problems. One structural pattern: we keep trying to fix the engine when the problem is the road.
This is the first episode in a new solo series on The Disruption Lab, where Kevin synthesizes the patterns emerging from the show’s conversations into frameworks for how innovation systems actually work.
