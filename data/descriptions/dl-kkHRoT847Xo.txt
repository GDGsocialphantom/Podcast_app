Why high-growth entrepreneurship is a different kind of economic infrastructure — and why most regions don't have a strategy for it.

Host Kevin McGinnis recently published an essay arguing that every startup is a headquarters. The data is compelling — but data alone doesn't capture what the distinction between high-growth and small business entrepreneurship actually looks like from inside an ecosystem. In this episode, Kevin goes deeper into the pattern he keeps seeing: we talk about entrepreneurship as one thing, fund it as one thing, and measure it as one thing. It isn't. A neighborhood restaurant and a venture-backed software company are both entrepreneurship — but they don't need the same capital, the same mentorship, or the same infrastructure. And when we treat them as one category, we underserve both.

Kevin breaks down the five stages of design that high-growth ecosystems need — from exposure to enterprise scale — and explains why most regions only build for one or two of them. He draws on the Moretti multiplier research, his two decades at Sprint watching transformational bets lose to safe ones, and the structural reality that makes this problem so persistent: small business is easy to champion and easier to measure. High-growth infrastructure is harder, more expensive, and politically inconvenient.
This isn't about choosing startups over small business. It's about recognizing that the people who pay the highest price for missing infrastructure aren't founders — they're the workers in every other sector who would have benefited from companies that never got built.
