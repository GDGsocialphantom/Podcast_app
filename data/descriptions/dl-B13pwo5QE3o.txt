Host Kevin McGinnis sits down with Kim Young, President of the Animal Health Corridor, to explore how Kansas City became the global capital of a $90 billion industry—and why almost nobody knows it.

In this episode:

The hidden infrastructure of food and medicine — How animal health connects to protein security, tariffs, and global food prices
Why consolidation matters — The shift from divisional pharma to standalone animal health companies (and what that means for investors)
The venture capital blind spot — Why early-stage animal health companies can't find funding, even though exits are happening
Where the real margins are — Companion animal vs. food animal economics, and why margins tell different stories
Technology that's actually disrupting — Direct-to-satellite livestock monitoring, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapy, AI in the clinic
The adoption problem entrepreneurs underestimate — Why your innovation fails when it disrupts century-old systems
Workforce gaps in plain sight — Why we have the talent but lack the pipeline, capital, and institutional alignment
The culture shift — How new leaders from CPG and consumer products are changing an industry that's been tight-knit for generations

Kim makes the case: Kansas City's greatest competitive advantage isn't what we're building next—it's what we're forgetting to protect and grow.

This episode is part of The Disruption Lab, a live-recorded podcast exploring how leaders navigate innovation, systems change, and the human cost of disruption.
