What happens when a founder builds a healthcare AI agent before checking whether it touches protected health information and finds out too late?

In this Keystone Sessions talk, Gwen Gillette breaks down the compliance framework that determines whether an AI agent in healthcare needs a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) at all and why most builders get the order of operations backwards. Instead of starting with a tool, Gwen walks through HIPAA's Treatment, Payment, and Operations (TPO) framework as the first filter for any healthcare AI project, then digs into what actually counts as de-identified data (it's not just stripping names; ZIP codes, the 18 required fields, and time-shifting all matter), and why "payment" is still the least-solved corner of healthcare AI.

The core of the talk is a side-by-side build: two AI agents from the same practice one flagging prior authorization denials (requires a BAA, touches PHI, needs a permanent human sign-off) and one identifying the slowest-paying insurers (no PHI, no BAA, runs almost anywhere). Gwen makes the case for which one a practice should actually build first, and why speed-to-value and regulatory exposure often point in opposite directions.

If you're building, deploying, or evaluating AI agents in a regulated industry, this is a working model for scoping compliance before you write a line of code.
