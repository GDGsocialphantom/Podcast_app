What if the thing holding your innovation back isn’t the idea but the story behind it?

On part one of three of this Keystone Session, Ron Green explains why innovation and storytelling follow the same path. Green spent 32 years at Hallmark Cards, where he founded Hallmark University and ran technical training for 1,400 creatives, then the largest in-house creative staff in the world. He’s now founder and executive director of the Digital Storytelling Center of Kansas City (DigiStory KC) and KC Imagine, the Kansas City Institute for Media, Animation, and Graphic Innovation and Education.

Green tells how he got hooked on digital storytelling in 1995, when Dana Atchley brought his interactive production Next Exit to Hallmark. He also tells the story of his first stop-motion animation workshop at Operation Breakthrough, which went wrong in three ways. The kids ate the fruit meant for animating, the laptops took over the room, and every “story” they turned in was a chase scene. That workshop led to a story model simple enough for a fourth grader to use. It turns out to work a lot like the innovation process:

→ Start with the want, not the hero
→ Something goes wrong, and the struggle begins
→ The universal flaw creates vulnerability and stakes
→ A turning point forces a choice
→ The new normal shows a universal truth

Green also covers why Kansas City’s digital media workforce matters, why visuals can improve learning by up to 400%, and DigiStory KC’s plans for the Laugh-O-Gram Studio, the building where Walt Disney started his first animation company.

This talk is for founders, innovators, educators and anyone building something new who needs people to care about it.
