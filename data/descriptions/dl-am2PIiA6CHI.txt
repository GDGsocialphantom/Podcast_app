In this solo episode of The Disruption Lab, host Kevin McGinnis makes an uncomfortable argument about the innovation economy — one aimed squarely at the field he works in. Everyone says ecosystems need more programming, more access, more capital. Kevin thinks we've been measuring the wrong thing almost entirely.
The companies that actually reshape a region were mostly built before they ever reached an accelerator, an incubator, or a grant program — outside of it, or beneath the things we bother to count. So the real question isn't whether the programming is good enough. It's harder than that: does any of it actually cause the outcome? Or does it just select the companies that were going to win anyway, then stand close enough to take the credit?
This is a clear-eyed teardown of selection vs. causation — and what an honest scoreboard for an innovation economy would actually look like.

▶️ In this episode:

— Why the resource we organize everything around (venture capital) never touched most high-growth companies

— The rigorous accelerator study that found the money and the workspace did nothing — and what actually moved the needle

— Why the real engine of a region sits off the org chart entirely

— How "being in the room" gets confused with "causing the outcome"

— The one counterfactual question almost no one in the field will ask out loud

— Why growth is designed, not accidental — but design is not the same as counting
