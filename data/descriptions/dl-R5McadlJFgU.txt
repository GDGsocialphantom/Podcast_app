Host Kevin McGinnis sits down with a corporate innovation leader to explore why corporate-startup engagement fails 80% of the time—and how to actually fix it.

What you'll learn:
The "corporate antibody" problem — How the same systems that protect a company's core business automatically kill startup ideas
Why procurement kills relationships before they start — The entry point matters more than the intention
Speed, timing, and cadence misalignment — Why a startup can build a prototype faster than a corporation can schedule a meeting
The ownership ambiguity problem — Who owns startup engagement inside big companies? Usually nobody.
Risk framing differences — Corporations manage downside. Startups manage upside. They're speaking different languages.
Why "innovation challenges" and "sponsored happy hours" don't work — Programming vs. infrastructure (and why infrastructure is what scales)
The reverse pitch case study — What happens when corporations bring real problems instead of startups pitching solutions
How the Sprint Accelerator created a parallel pathway — Building around antibodies instead of trying to change the immune system
The infrastructure that actually works — Innovation scout forums, corporate connectivity programs, and what's next

The real insight: Corporate startup engagement requires designed infrastructure, not good intentions. And when you build that infrastructure correctly, one Fortune 500 company shows up with seven real business problems and asks "when do we do this again?"

This episode is part of The Disruption Lab, a live-recorded podcast exploring how leaders navigate innovation, systems change, and the human cost of disruption.
