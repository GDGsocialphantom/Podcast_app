Kansas City spent years getting ready to welcome the world. What if the world had been here all along?

On this episode of The Disruption Lab, recorded live in front of a studio audience, host Kevin McGinnis sits down with Jessica Hong, chief curator of the Kemper Museum of Contemporary Art. She arrived in January 2025, in the final stretch of the city’s World Cup preparation. Everyone she met told her the world was coming. Then her studio visits turned up artists from Mexico, Guatemala, Germany, China, Japan, and Korea already working here, and she started asking why no one was talking about them.

That question became The World in Kansas City, an exhibition of 16 local artists from nine countries, with no soccer ball anywhere in it. Behind it was a research project into the region’s migration history, built with community historian Dr. Gene Chavez: Indigenous nations forcibly moved through the region, waves of industrial-era immigration, the Great Migration, and the Mexican Revolution. Along the way she surfaced history many longtime residents didn’t know. The Kansas City Art Institute dates to 1885. The Nelson-Atkins’ founding collection was Chinese and Asian art. And sculptor Alexander Archipenko, who worked alongside Picasso and Matisse in Paris, taught at UMKC in 1950–51, where two of his commissioned works still stand.
