This is a different kind of episode. Every solo I've done has been about a framework — a thesis, a system, an argument. This one is about a person.
Dan Hesse was my CEO at Sprint and became my mentor. He taught me that business and community are not separate concepts. I understood that as a value. It took me fifteen years to understand it as a design principle.
In this episode I tell the full story: what I watched Dan build at Sprint, what happened when it was taken away, and how losing it became the thing that eventually shaped everything I'm building now — from Keystone to the Social Venture Studio.
It's a story about mentorship, corporate responsibility, and one idea I keep coming back to: the outcomes you get are the outcomes your system was designed to produce. If your system separates business from community, it will produce growth that doesn't reach the people who need it most — not because anyone intended that, but because the architecture wasn't built to connect them.
If this resonates, the written version lives in my Field Note on Substack. And for anyone who wants to go deeper on the data behind doing well by doing good, check out JUST Capital — the research showing that companies measured by how they treat stakeholders, not just shareholders, create more durable value.
